The SBC stop defect, re-read — and yesterday’s version of it was wrong

Yesterday this page said SBC’s protective stops rested at the broker for roughly 1.44× the shares held in each account. Read again this morning against the brokers themselves, that is not what is there. The orders actually resting at each broker sum exactly to the holding: t-default 3,371 + 3,371 against 6,742 held; akul 489 + 376 against 865; t-ramprasad 698 + 698 against 1,396. What is 1.44× too large is this service’s own record of those stops, which carries a base leg equal to the full holding plus an add leg on top of it.

So the fault is a bookkeeping fault, not a live-order fault — the better of the two, and the opposite way round from how it was reported yesterday. It is corrected here in the same place it was got wrong. The reason it was got wrong is the standing one: a stop check that reads our own records is not a check of the broker, and the two tables disagreed. Nothing has been cancelled or amended at any broker.

⚠ The protection jobs are still not running, and the failure counts grew overnight

This is the most important thing on the page and it is not about the market. The scheduled jobs that trail stops, book fills, reconcile positions against the brokers and process exits have been failing continuously since 25 November 10:31. As of this morning: book-policy-fills has failed 855 consecutive times (585 yesterday), trail-stop-losses 49 (35 yesterday), reconcile-positions 28, book-confirmed-fills 28, pyramid-adds 18, with advance-book-policy, exit-book-policy, exit-broken-stops and settle-exits all overdue as well. The overnight scan job has been silent for 134 hours.

The cause is known and it is ours. Halting entries was done with a blanket pause across all three accounts, and the jobs that protect the open book read that same pause as “no account to work on” and refuse to run. Halting entries was never meant to halt protection. The open positions are not unprotected — every one of them has a stop resting at its broker right now, which is what the section above confirms — but nothing is moving those stops up as prices rise, and nothing is reconciling the book against the brokers automatically. Both have been done by hand this week instead.

The fix is one command and it is Divyansh’s to give, not something to take unilaterally: lift the blanket pause so the protection jobs run again, and leave the separate entry halt in place so nothing is bought. The two are different switches and only one of them was meant to be thrown. This has now been on the page two mornings running.

The trail was run by hand this morning, on all three accounts, at 08:42–08:46. Nine stops moved up: GCSL 777.48 → 782.30 and SBC 65.97 → 66.11 in every account, plus PTCIL and SHUKRAMED in akul and RATNAVEER in t-ramprasad. SHUKRAMED’s move closes the akul gap described two sections above — its structural level is no longer stale against its own cost. Five names had nothing to give: the level already resting sits above what the trail would compute, which for CUPID and SHUKRAMED means they are still pinned at their entry price while 7.3% and 8.9% ahead.

⚠ Three names refused the move, in all three accounts, for the same reason. AARTIPHARM, PVRINOX and ROSSTECH each came back placement rejected — quantity must be positive, got 0. These are the positions carrying a second, added leg, and the code that works out how many shares a replacement stop should cover computes zero for them. None of the three is unprotected — each kept the stop it already had, at 915.60, 1,551.00 and 1,375.00. But they cannot be trailed at all until the writer is fixed, and theirs were the two largest pending moves on the book: ROSSTECH would have gone to 1,417.74 and AARTIPHARM to 934.87, each about three percent of protection left on the table. This is a defect that was predicted on paper and has now been seen nine times live, and it is the second thing on this page needing a decision.

A sector's distribution count is weaker evidence than the market's, and knowingly so. The bars carry total cash-market volume rather than the instrument's own, so the 'on higher volume' half of a distribution day is a fact about the whole market and only the price half belongs to the sector. Read it as 'this sector fell on days the market traded heavily'.

The proxies are ETFs, not the indices. An ETF can trade at a premium and carries its own liquidity; on a thin day its close is a worse reading of the sector than the index's own would be. It is what the bhavcopy gives us.

A blank MarketSmith column means their mail did not name that index, which is not a disagreement. There is no published line for smallcap, midcap, Next 50, PSU bank or healthcare.
