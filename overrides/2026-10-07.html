A sector's distribution count is weaker evidence than the market's, and knowingly so. The bars carry total cash-market volume rather than the instrument's own, so the 'on higher volume' half of a distribution day is a fact about the whole market and only the price half belongs to the sector. Read it as 'this sector fell on days the market traded heavily'.

The proxies are ETFs, not the indices. An ETF can trade at a premium and carries its own liquidity; on a thin day its close is a worse reading of the sector than the index's own would be. It is what the bhavcopy gives us.

A blank MarketSmith column means their mail did not name that sector, which is not a disagreement. They publish no PSU-bank, healthcare or Next-50 line.

IPO review — apply, skip, avoid

Wednesday 7 October, written at 09:30. Nothing is open for application today and nothing is held. The September cohort has listed out and dropped off the source; the board is down to two forward names, neither of which has a published price band. That is the gap the 6 October footnote asked for, so this entry spends it on Jio Platform’s accounts rather than its grey-market quote — the largest IPO India has run, open 21–23 October. The numbers are now in: FY26 revenue ₹1,46,885 Cr (+14.6%), EBITDA ₹76,255 Cr at a 51.9% margin, PAT ₹30,049 Cr (+15.1%), 524.4 M subscribers, ARPU ₹214. At the reported ₹13 lakh crore equity value that is about 43× FY26 earnings for 15% profit growth. Two structural facts cut opposite ways and both matter: the issue is 100% fresh with no offer for sale — the one signal in an IPO that cannot be dressed, and the same one that made SRIT the pick last month — but ₹27,500 Cr of roughly ₹33,000 Cr goes to repaying borrowings, so it is a deleveraging, not an expansion. HD Fire Protect’s zero GMP is not a signal today and must not be read as one. Its band is not published until about 9 October, and a premium is a quote against a price: with no price there is nothing to quote. Our own calibration — a zero GMP has averaged −6.6% over 295 listings — is drawn from issues whose band existed and whose grey market declined to bid. This is not that, and treating the two as the same number is precisely the error this page made four times in September when it called thin institutional books decisive and was wrong every time. The one genuinely new argument for Jio is allotment odds, not price. On a ₹218 Cr issue a retail application is a lottery — SRIT closed at 91.84× QIB and nothing came. The retail quota of a ₹33,000 Cr issue is larger in absolute rupees by more than two orders of magnitude, so for the first time this quarter an application has a real chance of being filled. That changes the arithmetic of applying far more than a 14% grey-market premium does.

The quality is not in question &mdash; a 51.9% EBITDA margin on &#8377;1.47 lakh crore of revenue is a utility with a moat. The price is. Forty-three times earnings for 15% profit growth is a PEG near 2.9, and the growth that would justify it has to come from tariffs rather than subscribers: ARPU moved &#8377;206 to &#8377;214 in a year, under 4%, on a base of 524 million where there is not much of India left to add. Several houses expect the listing itself to trigger a tariff cycle, which is a reasonable thesis and an unproven one. <strong>Note what the fresh-issue structure does and does not say.</strong> No promoter is selling, which is genuinely rare at this size and removes the usual exit-at-the-top read. But 83% of the money repays borrowings. That lifts earnings by removing interest rather than by building anything, and a deleveraging is worth paying for at a lower multiple than a growth raise, not a higher one. No call today, by design: the band is the whole decision and it is not out.
