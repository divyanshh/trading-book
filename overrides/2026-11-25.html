The re-arming is the most consequential change this book has made in a month, and it is worth stating what it does and does not fix. It stops the service being sold by a wick: a position now has to close below its level to be exited, and Monday’s MCX and KRISHANA are two examples from a single session of trades that would have survived. It also roughly doubles the bad tail. A name that gaps straight through and keeps falling is now held to a floor 5% lower, on unchanged size, so the loss on that trade is about half as large again as it would have been. That trade-off was put to him explicitly and he took it. Post-exit drift is not a backtest of holding on, and this page will not pretend otherwise — the honest claim is narrow: the old rule was firing on the wrong event.

A sector's distribution count is weaker evidence than the market's, and knowingly so. The bars carry total cash-market volume rather than the instrument's own, so the 'on higher volume' half of a distribution day is a fact about the whole market and only the price half belongs to the sector. Read it as 'this sector fell on days the market traded heavily'.

The proxies are ETFs, not the indices. An ETF can trade at a premium and carries its own liquidity; on a thin day its close is a worse reading of the sector than the index's own would be. It is what the bhavcopy gives us.
