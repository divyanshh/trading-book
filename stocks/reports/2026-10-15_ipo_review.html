IPO review — apply, skip, avoid

Thursday 15 October, written at 11:20. Four issues opened this morning, HD Fire closes tonight, and with every prospectus now read the grey market has the cluster ranked almost exactly upside down. The highest premium on the board, Tonbo at 71.4%, belongs to the only company here whose revenue and profit both fell. The best set of accounts — Bonfiglioli, growing 29%, debt-free, 34% on capital — carries 30%. The loss-making one carries 15%. HD Fire is 16.51× subscribed with one session left, NII at 48.71× and retail at 11.36×, which is a strong book and also means retail allotment is now roughly a one-in-twelve lottery. Four prospectuses, one pattern. Ranked by what the accounts say, the order is Bonfiglioli, then Sahajanand, then Tonbo and Zetwerk nowhere. Ranked by what the grey market will pay, it is Tonbo, then Bonfiglioli, then Zetwerk, then Sahajanand. The only name the two orderings agree on is Bonfiglioli, and it is the one with no story attached — an Italian parent selling down a gearbox manufacturer. Tonbo is the live test of this page’s judgement and it is going the wrong way so far. The premium was 38.6% when the accounts were still unread, 61% when this page said no call belonged on it, 69.3% when the accounts arrived and the call became avoid, and 71.4% this morning. Nothing in the filings has changed: revenue down 22.7%, profit down 30%, a ₹17.74 Cr loss in Q1 FY27, operating cash flow negative in both of the last two years while profits were reported, and working capital out to 517 days. The call stands on those numbers and will be wrong if it lists well. Recording that now, before the print, is the only way the record is worth anything.

<strong>Set beside the other three, this is the only one that reads like a business rather than a theme.</strong> Profit up 29.2%, revenue up 17.9%, <em>no borrowings at all since CY24</em>, and return on capital employed that went from 24.53% to 34.05% &mdash; a company earning more on less. H1 FY27 EBITDA is already &#8377;223.87 Cr against &#8377;176.43 Cr a year earlier, so the growth is continuing into the year being sold, which is precisely what Tonbo cannot say. <strong>It is not cheap and it is still a full exit.</strong> 46.3 times at the top is a growth multiple, not a bargain, and every rupee of the &#8377;2,534 Cr goes to Bonfiglioli S.p.A. rather than into the company. A parent selling a profitable subsidiary at 46&times; is doing something rational for itself. But a 29% grower with no debt and 34% on capital is the only name on this board where holding past the listing is an argument rather than a hope, and it is carrying less than half Tonbo&rsquo;s premium.

<strong>Zetwerk is the only issue on this board raising real money and the only one losing it.</strong> &#8377;16,100.63 Cr of income for a loss of &#8377;1,606.17 Cr &mdash; a tenth of revenue, burned. The order book at &#8377;13,062 Cr is genuine and manufacturing EBITDA margin improved to 6.56% from 4.83%, so the direction is right; the distance is enormous. A business that must triple its margin to break even is not a listing-day trade and is not yet an investment. The grey market agrees for once, at 15.2%. <strong>Sahajanand is the more interesting of the two.</strong> Revenue up 18.11% to &#8377;1,210.50 Cr and profit from &#8377;25.15 Cr to &#8377;69.08 Cr &mdash; nearly tripled. Cardiac implants sold into 77 countries, with 73.87% of revenue earned outside India, which is both the strength and the currency risk. At roughly 53 times it is priced for that tripling to continue. The 12.2% premium is the lowest on the board, which is the market saying it does not believe it will.
