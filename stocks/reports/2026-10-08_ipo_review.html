IPO review — apply, skip, avoid

Thursday 8 October, written at 09:45. HD Fire Protect published its band and the picture changed. ₹258–271, opening Monday 13 October, and the grey market moved from nothing to ₹80 — 29.5% within a day of the price existing. Yesterday this page refused to read its zero GMP as bearish, on the grounds that a premium is a quote against a price and there was no price. That was the right call and the board has now said so. The issue itself is harder than its grey market. At the top of the band the company lists at ₹4,749 Cr against FY26 profit of ₹116.79 Cr — 40.7× earnings for 6.4% profit growth, and every rupee of the ₹712 Cr goes to the promoter couple selling it. Jio Platform’s premium keeps climbing — ₹185 now, from ₹167 yesterday and ₹160 on Monday — with the band still unpublished. Held: nothing in equities. Nothing is open for application today.

Two GMP sources disagree on HD Fire Protect today, one quoting ₹80 and one still showing zero, and that is worth stating rather than resolving by picking the one that suits. A grey-market quote has no print behind it; when the feeds diverge on the first day of a band, the honest reading is that the market has not settled, not that it has settled at the higher number. The structural case is the one this page keeps returning to. SRIT was worth applying for because every rupee went into the company. This is the inverse: a pure secondary sale by two promoters at forty times earnings, where the people who know the business best are the sellers and the proceeds build nothing. That is not disqualifying — a 30.75% EBITDA margin and Saudi Aramco on the customer list are real — but it means the entire case rests on the listing pop, and a listing pop is the one thing this page has been wrong about four times running when it tried to forecast it from the institutional book. Quote the QIB number on day two; do not predict it.
