The two issues on this page are the same structure and opposite businesses, and the grey market has them the wrong way round. Both are 100% offers for sale by existing holders; neither company receives a rupee. HD Fire earns 30% on equity and 40% on capital, converts its profit into cash, and asks 40.7× for 6.4% growth — expensive, which is why this page says sell it on listing rather than hold it. Tonbo asks roughly 79× for earnings that fell 30%, having burned cash in both of the last two years and with a quarter of losses already on the board. The premium on the second is more than double the premium on the first. That is what a theme does to a price. Defence has been the thing to own since Goodluck India was named sole supplier of 155 mm shells and Zen Technologies went after a ₹20,000 Cr tender, and Tonbo is the only listed-adjacent way to buy it this week. None of that is in the accounts. Four days ago this page wrote that a premium moving 58% in a session is information about sentiment and not about the company. The accounts have now confirmed which of the two it was.

<strong>This page said on 13 October that no call belonged on Tonbo until the revenue, the profit and the multiple were read, and that a 61% premium was a reason to hurry that rather than skip it. Here they are, and they are bad in every line that matters.</strong> Revenue did not slow, it <em>fell</em>: &#8377;469.08 Cr to &#8377;362.65 Cr, down 22.7%. Profit fell 30%, from &#8377;72.76 Cr to &#8377;50.88 Cr. <strong>Q1 FY27 is already a loss of &#8377;17.74 Cr</strong>, so the decline is continuing into the year being sold. <strong>The cash tells the sharper story.</strong> Operating cash flow was negative in <em>both</em> FY25 and FY26 while the company reported profits in both &mdash; profit that does not become cash &mdash; and working-capital days went 152, then 280, then 517. A business taking seventeen months to convert working capital is financing its customers, and 78.6% of FY26 revenue landing in the second half says the order flow is lumpy as well. <strong>At &#8377;700 the multiple is about 79 times earnings that are going backwards</strong>, for a &#8377;4,010 Cr valuation, in an issue where every rupee of the &#8377;852 Cr goes to existing holders and none to the company. The grey market is bidding +69%. The accounts and the premium are telling opposite stories and only one of them is audited.
