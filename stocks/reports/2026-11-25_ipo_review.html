Integris and Monomark have no band, so no percentage and no haircut are quoted for either. Static facts — splits, P/E, litigation, the H1 numbers — are carried forward from the 23 November cards rather than re-entered, so a correction to one corrects every day after it. The band is confirmed, so for the first time this issue can be judged. ₹181–191, ₹360.76 Cr in total, a lot of 78 shares — about ₹14,898 at the cap. The anchor book is allocated today and it opens tomorrow. The grey-market quote is ₹17 at investorgain's 24-Nov 20:02 print, which on ₹191 is 8.90%, or ~+6.3% after our 2.6-point haircut; ipowatch reads ₹16 (8.38%) — close enough that the two boards agree, and it is the high end of its own range, not a stale quote, which is the opposite of what this card said three times last week. The objection has not been answered and it is a real one. FY26 was a good year — PAT ₹14.26 Cr → ₹29.11 Cr, RoNW 17.35%, D/E 0.43 — but the six months to September lost ₹16.15 Cr on ₹222.18 Cr of income, with the EBITDA margin halving from 13.82% to 7.35%. Wedding and banquet catering is genuinely seasonal and the December-to-February half is where the money is made, so that loss may be the off-season rather than the business. It may equally not be, and nothing published so far settles it. On top of that, ₹168.76 Cr of the ₹360.76 Cr is selling shareholders taking money out. So: a listing trade, if he wants one, and explicitly not a holding. One lot, ₹14,898, against an expected ~+6.3% that is itself a haircut estimate — call it roughly ₹900 if the premium holds and a real loss if it does not, because there is no current-year profit underneath to catch it. That is a judgement about ₹15,000, and it is his to make, not this page's. The book runs to 30 November, so the decision is not due tonight — but the anchor allocation lands today and will tell us more than any grey-market quote will.

A cash machine with shrinking earnings, which is a dividend holding or nothing.

Carried so the board is complete, not because it is a candidate. It opens today on NSE Emerge, which is the SME platform, and the mandate this page works to is mainboard issues. The reason is not snobbery about small companies: an SME lot is 1,000 shares, about ₹1.42 lakh at the ₹142 cap, so a single application is roughly ten times a mainboard one and sits in the same size bracket as a real equity position — with a fraction of the disclosure and none of the liquidity. And the premium is not even agreed. ipowatch prints +₹10 (7.04%); investorgain prints ₹20 (14.08%).
