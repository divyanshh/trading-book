IPO review — apply, skip, avoid

Tuesday 10 November, written at 04:45 IST, before the open that will not come: the exchanges are shut today for Diwali Balipratipada and trading resumes Wednesday 11 November at 09:15. A closed session is not a reason to skip this page — a holiday is exactly when the next open gets decided — and this one earns its place twice over. The pipeline grew. Two mainboard names appear on the board that were not on the 9 November page: Avaada Electro at ~₹7,600 Cr, which is seven times the size of anything else queued, and Crystal Crop Protection. Both are TBA-TBA, as are Milestone Gears and Mahanadi Coalfields for a fourth consecutive session. And the 9 November Bombay Coated card was wrong. Its horizon field said the debut gain had held at ₹90.07; ₹90.07 was the 6 November close, two sessions stale when it was written. The stock has since fallen about 5% on each of the two sessions after it and closed 9 November at ₹81.30, below its ₹82 issue price. That is corrected in its card below. Nothing is open for subscription, nothing has an announced window for 11–21 November, and nothing lists in that span. Applied by this service: nothing, and nothing was available to apply for.

The whole fortnight, re-scored on the position rather than the print. Five mainboard debuts closed 9 November at: EAAA ₹1,695.50 (+1.83% on its ₹1,665 issue), BLS Polymers ₹237.45 (+58.3% on ₹150), SFC Environmental ₹409.30 (+24.0% on ₹330), M.K.Sons ₹154.56 (−4.59% on ₹162) and Bombay Coated ₹81.30 (−0.85% on ₹82). Every figure is the 9 November close, read from four independent quote pages that agree to the paisa; the lone figure here that is not is Crystal Crop’s presence on the board, which one aggregator carries and three do not. So the lesson of the 9 November footnote survives on the print and inverts on the position. On the listing print the 2.6-point haircut was short four times out of five and that stands unchanged — the premium was a floor, not a ceiling. On where the money actually is four to seven sessions later, two of the five are under water, and they are the two the haircut was blamed for under-calling. Bombay Coated listed +10.98% and is now −0.85%; M.K.Sons listed +5.56% and is now −4.59%. The 9 November page said of the Bombay Coated SKIP that ‘there is no reading of that which makes the SKIP right’. There is one, and it took three sessions to arrive: the position is below issue. The correction is the finding, not the embarrassment. The card was wrong because a price was quoted as ‘now’ when it was two sessions old, and the two sessions it missed were a Muhurat session and a holiday-shortened week — precisely the stretch where a quote page goes stale without looking stale. Any price written on this board from here carries the date it is a close for. backtest_gmp is still owed a re-run, and it should now be re-run against the five-session mark as well as the debut print, because those two numbers have disagreed in sign on 40% of this fortnight’s board. show_ipos is broken for a fourth session — ‘the page answered but carries no Current Mainboard IPO table at all’ — so an empty parse and an empty board still render identically and this page is still hand-read from live sources.

The one genuinely new fact on the board today, and the largest name in the pipeline by a factor of seven. Solar cell and module manufacturing. The structure is the thing to read before the band lands: <strong>₹6,000 Cr of ₹7,600 Cr is an offer for sale by the promoter</strong>, so only ₹1,600 Cr reaches the company, and ₹1,200 Cr of that is earmarked to repay SBI borrowings raised for the plants. On the published intent, roughly ₹400 Cr of a ₹7,600 Cr issue is new money for the business. That is the opposite of the SRIT structure this board called out on 26 September, where no OFS meant every rupee went into the company and could not be dressed up. FY26 numbers are genuinely strong — ₹5,304 Cr revenue, 23.7% EBITDA margin, ₹889 Cr PAT — and none of that is in question. The question the band will answer is what multiple is being asked for a business whose promoter is selling four rupees for every one it raises. <strong>No call is possible and none is made.</strong> Carried here so the first time this name needs a decision is not the first time it has been looked at.

<strong>This card replaces yesterday's, which was wrong, and the way it was wrong is the lesson.</strong> The 9 November page graded the SKIP as a clear miss and wrote in its horizon field that the debut gain &lsquo;has HELD: ₹90.07 now, +9.84% on issue, four sessions later&rsquo;. ₹90.07 was not now. It was the 6 November close, and between it and the moment that sentence was written sat the 8 November Muhurat session (₹85.57) — so the number was two sessions old and read as current. By that evening the stock closed ₹81.30, a third consecutive fall of about five percent, with a 9 November range of ₹81.30 to ₹81.30: it opened at the floor and did not trade off it. <strong>So the SKIP is right where it counts.</strong> Anyone who flipped on listing day made +10.98%; anyone who held the position is now down on a ₹82 issue. That is the identical shape to M.K.Sons, and it is now the second of the fortnight's five debuts to give the whole pop back. The honest statement of the record: <strong>the haircut under-called the print and the call out-lived the print</strong>, and yesterday's page conflated the two because it was reading a stale quote.
