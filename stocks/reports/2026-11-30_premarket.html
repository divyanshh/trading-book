“Small caps were up and the Nifty was down” — measured

Divyansh asked on Friday evening whether the book should turn toward small caps. The premise is correct and the conclusion does not follow, and the way to tell them apart is to measure the condition rather than the day.

Friday, as described. Nifty 50 −1.07%, Midcap 100 +0.10%, Smallcap 100 +0.56%. That is exactly the divergence he saw.

What that condition has been worth. A new backtest replays the exact rule — the Nifty fell and the smallcaps rose — across 345 sessions of the real NSE index archive, 7 July 2025 to 27 November 2026, and asks what the smallcap-minus-Nifty spread did afterwards. The condition fired on 49 of 344 sessions, 14.2% of them.

Past the first day it is negative at every horizon, negative in both halves of the sample, and nothing is close to significant. Ten thousand permutations that shuffle which days the signal picked out — keeping the return series exactly as it fell — beat the real signal between 29% and 47% of the time. A day on which the broad market outruns the index is a description of that day. The honest reading is not “rotate” and not “do the opposite” either; it is that this particular day carries no information about the next month.

One related signal did look real, and it fails the split that every finding on this site has to survive. “Smallcaps ahead over the trailing five sessions” showed a +0.83 gap at ten sessions (p = 0.0022) and +1.35 at twenty (p = 0.0038) — which, taken whole, reads like an edge. Split within periods it collapses: first half +0.09 (p = 0.74) and +0.14 (p = 0.74); second half +1.13 (p = 0.02) and +1.67 (p = 0.07). All of it is one regime. And it is not firing anyway — the trailing-five spread is currently negative. A cross-section is not evidence until it survives a within-period split, which is the rule that saved us here.

The window decides the answer, and that is itself the finding. Over the last 60 sessions the large caps led decisively — Nifty +6.34% against Midcap 150 −3.76% and Smallcap 100 −2.26%. Over calendar 2026, on our own closes, it is the exact reverse: non-index names returned +15.4% against index members’ +7.1%, measured in Thursday’s large-cap study. Both are true. Any rotation call that flips depending on which of those two windows you happen to pick is not a call, it is a window.

And the change is not one that is available to us. The scanned universe is 1,065 names — the breadth figure at the top of this page is computed over the 882 of them with enough history for a 20-day average, which is why the two counts on this page differ — almost none of them Nifty 50 — the book is already entirely small and mid cap. The other direction was measured on Thursday and earns nothing: all 980 large-cap rule combinations tested lost money, against +0.4% for simply holding the index members. So: no change on capitalisation, and none is proposed. The things actually binding are further down this page.

⚠ Three names cannot be trailed at all, and two parking funds have no stop recorded

AARTIPHARM, PVRINOX and ROSSTECH each refused their trail in all three accounts, for the same reason — placement rejected: quantity must be positive, got 0. These are the positions carrying a second, added leg, and the code that works out how many shares a replacement stop should cover computes zero for them. None of the three is unprotected: each kept the stop it already had, at 915.60, 1,551.00 and 1,375.00. But they cannot be moved up at all until the writer is fixed, and they have now failed eighteen times live across Friday’s two passes. The fix belongs in the writer, not in the rows — editing the stored quantities would hide the defect rather than end it.

LIQUIDCASE has no stop of any kind recorded in akul or t-ramprasad. The parking fund is not a trade and is not meant to carry one, so this is a bookkeeping gap rather than an exposed position — but it is the kind of gap a stop check that reads our own records would tick green over, and it is written down here for that reason. t-default’s parking does carry one, at 115.92.

And the standing one: a stop check that reads our own database is not a check. Everything above was read against the brokers themselves. Where our record and the broker disagree, the broker is what is quoted.

⚠ Seven faults in the rest of this page, found by reading it after it was built

The page below was reviewed line by line once it was published, and it carries faults that are worth knowing before any of it is read as fact. None of them is fixed here — they live in the renderer and in the data behind it — so they are listed instead.

  1. The one PASS row in the setups table contradicts its own linked audit. SHIVAUM shows 8 of 8 with a size of 884 shares and a deploy of ₹4,99,752. Its audit page, linked from that same row, says NOT A BUY — an ASM low-liquidity flag, a ₹736 Cr market cap, no institutional holders, and the scanner itself refusing it on a sub-gate on 4 November. The row also shows volume 98% below its fifty-day average. Nothing in that table is an instruction and nothing will be bought from it: the gate is shut and entries are halted by hand on top of that. TFCILTD has the same shape and is already held.
  2. The auto-generated IPO board says “no mainboard IPO is open or upcoming”. It sits directly above the hand-written review, which has Foodlink closing today and three more opening this week. The hand-written review is the one that was checked; the board above it is reading an empty table in the service.
  3. The institutional-flows table and chart are missing Thursday 26 November, a trading day. The five-session FII figure of +29,506 is the sum of the five rows shown, so it skips that day, and the November bar is understated by the same amount.
  4. The breadth history carries 24 November as a row with figures identical to 23 November. 24 November was an exchange holiday; that row is a carry-forward, not a reading.
  5. The MarketSmith buy-watchlist table has seven junk rows — newsletter prose swallowed by the parser, two of them duplicates, one rendering a literal escaped ampersand. The watchlist read in full further down this page is the one to use; it was read off the mail body, not off that parser.
  6. The news section’s ages are relative to Friday evening, not to Monday morning, and it is not ordered newest first. An item marked “2h ago’ is two hours before this page was built.
  7. “How the market opened” is Friday’s open, carrying Friday’s 09:08 GIFT Nifty quote, and says nothing about being stale. There is no Monday open yet.
