Three of those alpha figures are the same six months, and they disagree by 55 percentage points. That is the benchmark choice talking, not the trading. Against the Nifty 50 almost the whole smallcap premium is booked as skill; against Nifty Smallcap 100 — the index this book actually tracks, beta 1.02, R² 0.39 — it collapses to +1.2%. None of the four is statistically significant (largest |t| = 1.22 against the 1.96 needed), so all of them are better read as zero than as a result.
the finding

Beta, executed well — not alpha

₹20 L returned +24.0% in under six months while Nifty Smallcap 100 did much the same. The book was well paid for its risk: Sharpe 1.66, Sortino 2.38, Calmar 6.35, and it beat holding government bonds by ₹4,16,643.

Against the index it actually trades, though, it delivered exposure rather than edge. Beta to Nifty Smallcap 100 is 1.017 with t = 8.4 — among the most solidly measured numbers a six-month sample can produce — while Jensen’s alpha is +1.19% with t = 0.04, which is zero. R² of 0.39 is the highest of any benchmark tested, which is the statistical way of saying smallcap is what this book rides.

Read plainly: the return came from being fully exposed to smallcaps through a smallcap rally, executed with good risk control. That is a real achievement, and it is not the same claim as alpha.

This page and the 14 October risk review disagree, and the difference is the capital base. That page reports +9.38% and beta 0.372 against the same index over a slightly different window; its return basis implies a denominator roughly 2.5× the ₹20 L used here and on the performance chart. Returns and beta both scale inversely with assumed capital, so the two describe the same book against different bases rather than disagreeing about what happened.

in plain language

What each of these means, and whether ours is good

Standard deviation (volatility)26.6% a year

How much the book’s value jumps around day to day, annualised. Pure movement — it does not care whether the movement is up or down.

High, but so is what it tracks. This is a concentrated smallcap book, and 26.6% against the index’s own volatility is the price of the return rather than a fault.

Sharpe ratio1.66

Return above the risk-free rate, divided by volatility. “How much reward did each unit of wobble buy?” Above 1 is good, above 2 is strong, below 0 means a fixed deposit beat you.

Good, close to strong. 1.66 is a number most funds would be pleased with. The book earned about ₹4.17 L more than government bonds would have paid on the same ₹20 L.

Sortino ratio2.38

Same as Sharpe, but only counts downward wobble as risk — being punished for upside volatility is unfair, so this is the fairer version.

Strong. Above 2, and well above the Sharpe — most of the volatility was upside. The book moved hard in the right direction more often than the wrong one.

Maximum drawdown−9.56%

The most the account fell from its own high. Not a hypothetical entry at the top — this is money that was on the screen and then was not. You lived through it.

Mark-to-market, not realised. On booked P&L alone the worst looks like −10.46%, but the account gained ₹1.47 L across those dates — losers cut, winners still running. Realised-only drawdown misleads for a book built this way; this is the real figure.

Calmar ratio6.35

Annual return divided by the worst drawdown. “How much return per unit of worst-case pain?” Above 1 is respectable, above 3 is excellent.

Excellent. 6.35 is a strong ratio of return to worst-case pain. Mild caution: Calmar rests on a single worst episode, and six months holds few of them.

Beta1.02 vs smallcap

How much the book moves when the market moves. 1.0 means it tracks the index; 0.5 means half as much; 0 means it ignores the index entirely.

Essentially 1.0 — the book IS smallcap exposure. t = 8.4, the most solidly measured number on this page. It moved one-for-one with the index it trades.

Alpha (Jensen’s)+1.2% to +56.0%

The return left after accounting for whatever the market handed you for free — the part you can claim as skill, and the number fund managers are judged on. It is always measured against a benchmark, and the benchmark is a claim about what you could have done instead.

Against the broad market the book won decisively: alpha of +56.0% against the Nifty 50, which returned +2.6% over the same six months, and +37.7% against the Nifty 500 (+8.2%). Against the smallcap indices it tracks most closely it is +1.2% (Smallcap 100) and +2.2% (Smallcap 250). The spread is the benchmark choice rather than the trading: a smallcap book measured against a largecap index books the entire size premium as skill. On this sample none of the four clears significance — largest |t| is 1.22 against the 1.96 needed — so each is better read as promising than proven. Six months is too few observations; the same figures over two years would settle it.

t-statistic0.04 on alpha

How confident we can be that a number is not luck. |t| above 1.96 means 95% confident it is real; below that you cannot distinguish it from random.

The honest brake on the alpha above. The beta carries t = 8.4, so the exposure is a measured fact; the skill is not. More sessions is the only cure.

R²0.39 vs smallcap

What share of the book’s movement is explained by the index. 1.0 means it is the index; 0 means it does its own thing.

39% came from the smallcap index, 61% from the specific stocks picked — the highest R² of any benchmark here, which confirms smallcap is the right yardstick for this book.

Tracking error20.8% vs smallcap

How far the book’s returns drift from the benchmark’s, annualised. Low means you hug the index; high means you go your own way.

High — nothing like an index fund. The drift was roughly neutral in net effect: wide swings either side of the index that came out about even.

Information ratio0.09 vs smallcap

Return earned above the benchmark, per unit of how far you strayed from it. Above 0.5 is good, above 1 is excellent.

Essentially zero. Straying from the smallcap index neither helped nor hurt. Against Nifty 500 (1.45) and Nifty 50 (1.80) it looks strong, but those are not what this book trades.

method

What to trust, and what to distrust

Measured, not reconstructed. The daily series is the one behind the performance chart: 2,373 broker fills across the full equity account, marked to each day’s close, with profit moved from unrealised to realised as positions are sold. Realised P&L reconciles to dated broker exports.

Full account, not strategy-only. The series covers the whole equity account rather than the narrower algo subset, so these figures describe the account, not the strategy in isolation.

Half a year is a short sample. 115 return observations. A Sharpe estimated over this window carries a wide confidence interval, and square-root-of-time annualisation assumes returns are serially independent, which trend-following returns often are not. Treat the annualised figures as scaling, not forecasts.

Costs are excluded. Before brokerage, STT and slippage. Across 2,373 fills that is a real drag, so every return figure here is optimistic by some margin.

Index levels from the NSE index archive. Risk-free 7.15%, India 10Y G-sec, 10 November 2026. The book was restarted on 1 October; that period is reported separately.
