IPO review — apply, skip, avoid

Tuesday 13 October, written at 09:20. HD Fire Protect opens this morning, and the anchor book answered the question this page set yesterday. It called the anchor allocation “the first verifiable number this issue has produced”, and it came in strong: ₹213.11 Cr from 25 funds, allotted at the top of the band at ₹271, with Indian mutual funds taking 71.88% of it. That is the full anchor allocation taken up, by domestic institutions, at the highest price on offer. The other half of the board moved the opposite way overnight. Tonbo Imaging’s premium went from ₹270 to ₹427, a 61% expected listing gain, while Zetwerk’s halved from ₹30 to ₹16 and Bonfiglioli, Sahajanand and Fusion CX all softened. One name is being bid and the rest are being sold. Held: nothing in equities.

What the anchor book does and does not settle. It settles that institutions would pay ₹271 for HD Fire in this tape — 25 of them, mostly domestic mutual funds, taking the whole allocation at the ceiling rather than the floor. It does not settle the valuation, which is unchanged: forty times earnings for 6.4% profit growth, margins compressing, a cash conversion cycle stretched to 102 days, and both sellers the promoters taking the entire ₹712 Cr out. This page has been wrong about institutional demand before — it called thin QIB books decisive four times in September and was wrong four times — and the lesson taken was to quote those numbers rather than forecast from them. So: the number is strong, it is quoted, and it is a reason to apply rather than a reason to hold. Tonbo is the one to be careful about. A 61% grey-market premium on an issue that is entirely an offer for sale, at a ₹4,010 Cr valuation, with no published profit figure this page has yet read, is not a bargain being spotted. It is a theme being bought — defence, in the week Goodluck India was named sole supplier of 155 mm shells and Zen Technologies chased a ₹6,000 Cr tender. The accounts are owed tomorrow, before the 15 October books open, and no call belongs on it until they are read.

<strong>A premium that moves 58% in one session is information about sentiment, not about the company</strong>, and the company has not published anything in that window. Nothing changed at Tonbo overnight; what changed is that defence became the thing to own, in the same week Goodluck India&rsquo;s arm was named sole supplier of 155&nbsp;mm M1A4 shells to Munitions India and Zen Technologies was reported chasing &#8377;6,000 Cr of a &#8377;20,000 Cr tender. <strong>The structure is the part that is knowable and it is the unflattering half.</strong> &#8377;852 Cr, every rupee of it an offer for sale of 1.22 Cr shares, at a valuation of &#8377;4,010 Cr. The company raises nothing and builds nothing; existing holders are selling into enthusiasm. That is not disqualifying on its own &mdash; HD Fire is the same shape and this page is applying for it &mdash; but HD Fire has published accounts and an anchor book, and Tonbo has neither here yet. <strong>No call until the revenue, the profit and the multiple are read.</strong> That work is owed tomorrow, which leaves one clear session before the book opens on the 15th. A 61% premium is a reason to hurry it, not a reason to skip it.
