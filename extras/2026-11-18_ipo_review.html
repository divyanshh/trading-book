<strong>The anchor book was filed at 18:55 on 17 November, and it argues against the grey market rather than with it.</strong> ₹135.96 Cr from <b>17 investors at ₹272, the top of the band</b>, with <b>44.28% taken by domestic mutual funds</b> — Tata, WhiteOak and Groww — and <b>IFC in for ₹20 Cr</b>. Domestic funds putting up nearly half of an anchor book at the top of the band is not what a deal in trouble looks like, and the premium agreed: <strong>it re-rated from ₹14 to ₹18 within an hour of the filing</strong>. Two things keep the call where it is. <b>No insurer and no pension fund took any of it</b> — the stickiest money stayed out, and a book carried by mutual funds and one development lender is a narrower endorsement than the headline number. And nothing in the filing touches the case itself: priced above the listed-peer median on real full-year earnings while marketed at 8x, litigation of ₹341.95 Cr against net worth, and ₹270 Cr of the fresh money repaying borrowings that rose from ₹820 Cr to ₹952 Cr in a quarter. <strong>So: a better book than the ₹45 → ₹13 collapse implied, on a company still priced for more than it has shown.</strong> Odds, said as odds: retail is at least 35% of the issue, so a one-lot application has fair allotment odds; at an expected +4.0% after the haircut a one-lot win is worth about ₹600, and the error band is still wider than the figure.
