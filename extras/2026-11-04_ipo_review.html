The haircut failed twice yesterday, and in the direction it is not supposed to fail. The rule is GMP% minus 2.6 points, the overstatement backtest_gmp measured over 295 listings (r = 0.87). On 3 November both listings came in above their raw GMP, so subtracting made the estimate worse, not better. BLS Polymers: GMP 46.7%, expected +39 to +44% after the haircut, listed +65.33% — the estimate was short by 21 to 26 points. SFC Environmental: GMP 4.5%, expected about +1.9%, listed +7.61% and traded to +29.1% intraday — short by 5.7 points at the open and by 27 at the high. Two listings is not a sample and this does not retire the haircut; the measured spread around it was already −3 to +7.5 points, and +21 is outside even that. What it does mean is that today’s small premiums cannot be read as ‘flat listing’ with any confidence. M.K.Sons at 3–4% and Bombay Coated at 10–11% are being estimated by a rule that was just wrong by more than either number. Subscription figures. Bombay Coated’s trackers still do not agree, and the spread has widened rather than settled: 63.89×, 32.68× and 3.78× are all in print for the same closed book. The 63.89× figure appears on more than one page and matches IPOji’s 2 November 17:00 stamp, so it is the one carried on the card — but a 17× disagreement between trackers is itself the finding, and no exchange consolidated figure has been read on this pass. M.K.Sons closed at about 5.09×, which is the only figure any tracker prints. Grading: BLS and SFC are graded below at their 3 November listing. M.K.Sons is graded on tomorrow’s page, Bombay Coated on 5 November’s.

The NO stands on its own evidence: a book that never cleared institutional demand, a premium that halved across four days, and a sector the report put at WEAKENING. None of that changed overnight. What changed is the confidence attached to the <em>expected listing</em> figure beside it. Yesterday both listings beat their GMP outright, so a 3 to 4 percent premium on a 162 rupee cap should not be read as a guaranteed flat open. It is still a NO — but it is a NO on the book and the sector, not on the arithmetic, and that distinction was not drawn clearly enough on yesterday&rsquo;s page.

Nothing here is actionable and the card exists to carry the number forward to tomorrow&rsquo;s grade. Two things are worth writing down. This is the grade that matters and it is a bad one. We called NO on a 4.5 percent premium, described it as falling, and said the expected listing was not distinguishable from flat. It opened 7.61 percent up and touched 29.12 percent intraday. Three separate things went wrong and they should be named separately. The issue was <strong>omitted from the 2 November page</strong> and only restored on the 3rd. Its 9.47 times book was treated as weak demand when it carried strong QIB and NII participation. And the GMP, which we read as decaying, understated the open rather than overstating it &mdash; the same failure as BLS on the same day, which makes two out of two. A rule that is zero for two on the only day it was tested in public is not a rule to quote confidently on today&rsquo;s cards, and the footnote says so.
