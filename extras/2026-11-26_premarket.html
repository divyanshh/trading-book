Pre-session read

Thursday 26 November, written before the open on Wednesday’s close. The Nifty rose 1.85% to 25,819.25 — its best session in weeks — and the market got narrower, not broader. The share of the scanned universe holding above its own 20-day average fell again, to 42.52% against a five-session mean of 56.90%. That is the lowest breadth reading this page has ever recorded, lower than Monday’s 46.15%, and it is the eighth consecutive session on which the market half our setups live in has failed its own breadth test.

Against that: 78.0% of the Nifty 50 closed above its own 20-day average, and 43 of the 50 are above their 50-day average. Forty-two of the fifty rose on Wednesday against eight that fell. On the same day, the Smallcap 100 fell 0.44%.

Both of those numbers are now on this page, side by side, for the first time. Until today the report carried one breadth line, computed across everything scanned, and it has been saying “no” for eight sessions while the index printed green days. The second line, computed over the Nifty 50 alone, is what the index has actually been doing. A 35-point gap between the two is not a market with one story in it. Nothing was bought on this reading and nothing is wired to it — it is being recorded first and judged later, which is the rule here for any new measurement.

New on this page: a second breadth reading, and a large-cap board

Three things were added to this report overnight, and this is the first morning they appear. The first is a second market-breadth line computed over the Nifty 50 alone, drawn beside the existing one, which is computed over the whole scanned universe. The two have been telling different stories for three weeks and until today the page only carried one of them. The second is a large-cap board: every Nifty-50 name trading above its own 50-day average, with how far it sits from its 20-day and 10-day averages, how far it is below its own 52-week high, its sector and its latest headline, ranked nearest its own high first. The third is a candle chart for each of those names, drawn the same way the TIS setups are drawn. None of it is a buy list and none of it is wired to the gate. It is there to be measured first: the rule here is that a reading earns a gate only after it has been recorded long enough to be tested, and this one has one day of history. The index membership is NSE’s own ind_nifty50list.csv, read on 26 November, and the page says so rather than guessing at “the fifty largest”.
