The premium sits at ₹2–3 against ₹136, under 2%, which is below our 2.6-point haircut and therefore not a listing gain at all. Note the premium history is contested: our board has carried ₹25 on 13 November, and the GMP trackers put the peak at ₹9.25 on the 14th. Either way the direction is the same and the level is now immaterial. India's largest coal producer by volume, debt-free, 43.6% EBITDA margin, PAT drifting ₹11,842 Cr → ₹10,825 Cr → ₹10,698 Cr with H1 FY27 annualising ~9% lower. A cash machine with shrinking earnings, sold entirely by a government seller into retail indifference. If he wants it he wants the dividend, and the Coal India shareholder quota of up to 10% is the only real edge — a portfolio decision, not an IPO one.

Final day, and the demand is real. The issue closed day two at 3.07x — retail 3.29x, NII 3.57x, QIB 2.23x — which is a genuinely subscribed book rather than a deal being walked over the line. Sources disagree on the intraday path (1.27x at 13:32, 1.94x at 11:22, 3.07x at 17:04), so the only figure worth quoting is the end-of-day one. The premium did not follow it up. ₹17–18 against ₹19 on the 18th: a subscribed book that the grey market will not re-rate. On a ₹272 band that is about 6.4%, and under 4% once our measured 2.6-point overstatement comes off. The call does not move, because the subscription was never the objection. The objection is the company — priced above the listed-peer median on real full-year earnings while marketed at 8x, ₹341.95 Cr of litigation against net worth, and ₹270 Cr of the fresh money repaying borrowings that grew ₹820 Cr to ₹952 Cr in a quarter. A well-sold book on a fully-priced company is still a fully-priced company.
