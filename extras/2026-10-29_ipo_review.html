The Jio sell call, and what was actually wrong with it. It was given at about 10:30 with the stock at ₹1,357, the low of its range at that point, and the reasoning leaned on exactly that: listed 1,358, high 1,374.5, back at 1,357, supply meeting the open. The stock then closed around ₹1,421. The structural half of the argument still holds — ₹27,500 Cr of the raise repays debt, a day-one listing has no derivable stop, and our own gate was shut. Selling a listing pop remains the policy and it is not being rewritten after one session. What was wrong was the evidence used to time it: a sixty-minute range read as distribution. An hour of tape on a listing day is noise, and quoting it as though it were a signal dressed a policy decision up as a market read. The call should have been made on the policy alone, which would have reached the same answer without claiming to know what the afternoon would do. The GMP record, now three listings deep: HD Fire implied +29% and paid +23.62%, Tonbo implied +64% and paid +66%, Jio implied +22.1% and paid +21.4% at the open and +27% at the close. The 2.6-point haircut is a mean correction being used as a point forecast, and the spread around it is wider than the haircut itself.
