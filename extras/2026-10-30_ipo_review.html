How the allotment odds are worked. In an oversubscribed retail or NII category, every allottee gets the category’s minimum application, chosen by lottery. The odds for one minimum application are therefore no worse than 1 ÷ (subscription multiple). They are better when applicants bid above the minimum, and no source here prints application counts per category. An undersubscribed category is filled in full, at whatever size was bid. Bidding above the minimum in sHNI blocks more capital and does not improve the odds. All multiples are 29 Oct closes and will rise through today; the odds can only shorten. Minimum applications at the cap price: Bombay Coated: retail 1 lot ₹14,924; sHNI 14 lots ₹2,08,936; bHNI 68 lots ₹10,14,832. M.K.Sons: retail 1 lot ₹14,742; sHNI 14 lots ₹2,06,388; bHNI 68 lots ₹10,02,456. Both bHNI minimums are above the ₹5,00,000 per-trade deploy cap, so bHNI is breached before any view on the issue. Against the ₹20,000 flat risk, an sHNI allotment of Bombay Coated uses the whole budget if it lists about 9.6% below issue (₹20,000 ÷ 2,548 shares = ₹7.85 a share). The call is not what was wrong. One retail lot at ₹15,000 into a 45% premium is still the right side of this. What was wrong is the number this page exists to state honestly: the chance of getting any of it. The rule that follows: a subscription figure is only usable with the hour it was read, a mid-window figure is never the basis of an odds statement, and the last day is when institutions bid — so any book quoted before the close is a floor, not a measurement.
